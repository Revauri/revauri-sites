# Blog content backlog and SEO research

Research date: **2026-07-27**. Method: manual SERP checks on real Google results. No paid keyword tools were used, so **search volume is inferred, not measured** — competition and SERP composition are directly observed and are the reliable part of this document.

Re-verify SERPs before committing to a topic more than ~6 months after the date above. Rankings move.

---

## The one rule that matters

Revauri's domain is new and has few backlinks. It cannot outrank high-authority generalists (Forbes Advisor, Clutch, HubSpot, WebFX, Healthline, Investopedia) or entrenched vertical specialists with years of topical coverage.

**The test that predicts winnability:** look at whether page one is *fragmented* or *repetitive*.

- **Fragmented** — different small boutiques ranking for each sub-niche query. Nobody owns the vertical. There's room. → Legal looks like this.
- **Repetitive** — the same handful of specialist shops appearing across every sub-niche. They've built authority over the whole topic map. → Every rejected vertical looks like this.

Apply this test to any new topic before writing.

## Rejected verticals

All four were rejected on **cost/design-cost queries specifically**. A separate research pass on non-pricing angles (compliance, page speed, platform migration, data portability) was run — see its findings before assuming these are permanently closed.

| Vertical | Why rejected | What would change it |
|---|---|---|
| **Medical & dental** | Same ~6 shops repeat across dental, med spa, dermatology, orthodontics, general practice: Flamingo Agency, PatientGain, GMR Web Team, Rosemont Media, Dentive, TargetPatientsMD. ADA.org also ranks. Competitors also sell subscription-only ($399–999/mo, no upfront build) — a different sales motion than Revauri's. | One real clinic or med-spa build for proof, then target a single narrow sub-niche rather than "medical" broadly. |
| **Home services** | builtrightdigital.com ranks page-one for *every* trade checked — a programmatic content operation across the whole niche. Plus ServiceTitan, HomeAdvisor, WebFX. | Little would. The deeper problem is channel: this buyer skews template/DIY budget and arrives via Angi/Thumbtack/word-of-mouth, not organic search for custom design. A winnable SERP still wouldn't reach a buyer who isn't looking. |
| **Professional services** | One dominant vertical SaaS per niche: cpasitesolutions (CPAs), advisorwebsites (financial advisors — the most entrenched SERP found in any round), insurancesplash (insurance). Consulting has no distinct SERP at all; it collapses into generic small-business queries owned by Forbes Advisor and Squarespace. | Vertical client proof, plus an angle the subscription incumbents can't answer honestly. |
| **Senior care** | Locked by vertical specialists (Thrive Agency, Straight North, Vision Design Group, IlluminAge, Blue Interactive, CCR Growth). | Also a **proof mismatch**: Ultaura is a consumer AI app bought by families; senior-living web buyers are B2B facilities and home-care agencies. Citing Ultaura here would be a credibility liability, not an asset. |
| **Crypto / fintech** | **Intent mismatch**, not competition. Everyone searching "crypto website cost" wants to build an exchange or payment product, not buy a marketing site — SERP is dev shops quoting infrastructure builds (galaxyweblinks, merehead, hashcodex, suffescom, innowise, coinsclone). Ranking would attract non-buyers. | A fundamentally different framing. Cryptrac backs fintech technically, but the query space doesn't contain Revauri's buyer. |

**Do not write for a vertical with no portfolio proof.** The content won't rank without credibility, and publishing it would be overclaiming. The path into any rejected vertical is landing one client there first — even discounted — then writing.

## Writing standards for this blog

Derived from what made page one beatable in the first place: competitors publish vague, interchangeable content.

- **Be specific enough to be useful to someone who won't hire you.** Concrete numbers, named tradeoffs, and honest cases where the cheaper option is right.
- **No fabricated statistics.** No "studies show." If a number appears, it must be sourced and linked, or explicitly labeled a general market range.
- **Never attach a price to a named client.** Case studies prove *build quality*, never what someone paid. Clients are identifiable.
- **Keep Revauri's own numbers off the page.** Pricing is quote-only by design; link to `/pricing` instead.
- **Only claim what `lib/portfolio-data.ts` actually records.** No implied traffic lifts or case outcomes that aren't documented.
- **Every post needs internal links** — the relevant case study plus `/pricing` — and one CTA to `/book`.
- **Skip the throat-clearing.** No "in today's digital landscape." Open with something the target reader would keep reading past.

## Measuring whether this is working

Watch **impressions in Search Console**, not clicks or sessions, for the first 90 days. Impressions rising means Google is starting to trust the pages; clicks lag well behind.

Use the Queries report to find terms ranking on pages 2–5 and write the next post against those — the highest-leverage use of the data.

If impressions are flat after 90 days at a weekly cadence, **topic selection is wrong, not volume** — and that's the point at which service and location landing pages become the better investment than more posts.
